# Welcome to Magpie

Magpie helps you get organised with the files already on your
computer — photos, videos, scans, PDFs, whatever you keep. You create
a **project** (a single file on disk), point it at some folders, and
Magpie shows you every file inside. From there you can:

- Give a file a **name**, **rename** it, and add **tags** — short
  labels like `beach`, `Christmas`, or `contract` — so you can find
  groups of files in seconds later on.
- Double-click any picture to open the **magnifier** and page through
  the rest with the arrow keys.
- Look at everything the file already knows about itself — resolution,
  camera, when it was taken, page count for PDFs — right next to the
  file.
- Delete the ones you don't want any more.

Everything stays on your computer. Nothing is uploaded, no account is
needed, and you can keep using Magpie with no internet connection.

## Why use Magpie?

Most people end up with files scattered across folders like
`Camera Roll`, `iPhone Backup 2019`, and `Family - Copy (2)`. Windows
gives you those folders and not much more. Magpie gives you a proper
way to sort through them:

- **Find things fast.** Type `beach` in the search box and every file
  you tagged with `beach` shows up, no matter which folder it's in.
- **Nothing gets moved or modified inside the folders you add.** Your
  files stay exactly where they are on disk, byte-for-byte unchanged
  (unless you explicitly rename one). Tags and titles are saved
  inside your **project file**, which you choose the location of.
- **Multiple projects.** Keep one project for family photos and
  another for work receipts — they live in separate files and you can
  switch between them from the Project menu.
- **One tool for all your files.** Photos, videos, and documents all
  live in the same project, side by side, with the same tag search.

## A quick tour, in under two minutes

1. **Create a project** the first time you launch (or open an existing
   one from **Project → Open Project…**).
2. **Click "+ Add folder"** at the top of the window and pick a
   folder full of files. Magpie gets to work reading everything it
   finds.
3. **Scroll** through the files in the big central area — it stays
   smooth even with tens of thousands.
4. **Double-click** any picture to open the magnifier; use ← / → to
   flip through neighbours and Esc to close.
5. **Click a file** to open the details panel on the right. Type a
   title. Add a tag. Rename the file. Everything saves as you go —
   there's no "Save" button to remember.
6. **Hold Ctrl while clicking** to pick several files at once, then
   add the same tag to all of them.
7. **Tick tags in the left sidebar** to combine them with AND logic,
   or type in the search box to find files by title / filename.

That's the whole app in a nutshell. The rest of this manual explains
each part in a bit more detail.

## What Magpie is *not*

- **Not an editor.** It won't crop, straighten, retouch, or convert
  your files. Use another app for that.
- **Not a backup service.** Your files stay on your computer. If your
  hard drive fails, Magpie can't help — copy your files somewhere
  safe (an external drive, OneDrive, etc.).
- **Not a face-recogniser.** It won't guess who's in your photos or
  auto-tag them.

If you want any of those, Magpie is happy to sit next to whichever
tool does them for you — it won't get in the way.
